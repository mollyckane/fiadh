Resource Hub

Guides, links, and support for freelance artists in Ireland.

Tax & Registration

Getting started

Registering as a Sole Trader

If you earn income as a freelance artist in Ireland, you are legally required to register as a sole trader with Revenue. You can do this online via MyAccount. Registration is free and takes around 10 minutes.

Visit Revenue.ie
VAT

VAT Thresholds for Artists

As a freelance artist, you must register for VAT only if your annual income exceeds €40,000 for services. Cultural services may qualify for VAT exemptions worth checking.

Revenue VAT guide
Self-assessment

Filing your Form 11

Self-employed people in Ireland file a Form 11 each year by 31 October. Your income minus allowable expenses is your taxable profit. Keep receipts for everything.

Self-assessment guide
Artists exemption

Artists' Exemption Scheme

Revenue operates an artists' exemption allowing qualifying creative work to be exempt from income tax up to €50,000 per year. Applies to original, creative work in categories including visual art, writing, and music.

Check eligibility

Funding & Grants

Primary funder

Arts Council of Ireland

The main government body for funding the arts in Ireland. Offers bursaries, project awards, and travel grants for individual artists at all career stages. Check their opportunities calendar regularly.

View current opportunities
Local funding

Local Development Companies

Local Enterprise Offices (LEOs) across Ireland offer small business grants, mentoring, and training. Some programmes specifically target creative sector applicants.

Find your LEO
Creative industries

Screen Ireland & Creative Ireland

Creative Ireland's Creativity Fund and Screen Ireland both support project-based creative work. Deadlines vary; sign up for their newsletters to stay informed.

Artist Rights

Copyright

Copyright in Ireland

In Ireland, copyright is automatic from the moment you create original work. You do not need to register. Copyright generally lasts 70 years after the creator's death under the Copyright and Related Rights Act 2000.

Gov.ie copyright info
Moral rights

Moral Rights

As an artist you hold moral rights to be identified as the creator of your work and to object to derogatory treatment. These rights exist separately from economic copyright and cannot be sold, only waived.

Artists Ireland
Contracts

Why You Need a Written Contract

A written contract protects both you and your client. It should clearly state the scope of work, payment terms, deadlines, revision limits, and who owns the final work. Fiadh includes a contract template generator.

Go to generators

Pricing Your Work

Rates

Minimum Pay Guidance

Visual Artists Ireland and Create Ireland publish recommended minimum fees for artists working with publicly funded organisations. These are a useful baseline even for private commissions.

View recommended fees
Formula

How to Calculate Your Hourly Rate

A simple starting point: add up your annual living costs and business expenses, divide by the number of billable hours you realistically work per year (typically 1,000 to 1,400 for freelancers), then add a profit margin.

Negotiation

Asking for What You Are Worth

It is normal to feel uncomfortable quoting prices. Remember: your price includes your skill, time, equipment, and expertise built over years. Send a written quote before starting any work.
